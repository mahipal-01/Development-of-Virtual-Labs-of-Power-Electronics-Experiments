Single Phase Half Wave Controlled Rectifier with R-L-E Load

Read the overview before starting — understand the experiment, components, waveforms and outputs.

Press Read Aloud to hear this section

🔬What is this experiment?

A Single-Phase Half Wave Controlled Rectifier with R-L-E Load converts single-phase AC into a controlled DC output using a single SCR (Silicon Controlled Rectifier). The load consists of a resistor R, inductor L, and a back-EMF source E (representing a DC motor or battery).

Unlike the purely resistive case, the inductor stores energy and causes the SCR to keep conducting beyond the natural zero-crossing of the supply — up to the extinction angle β, where the instantaneous current finally returns to zero.

⚡How does it work?

The SCR fires when two conditions are met: the supply voltage Vmsinα must exceed the back-EMF E, and a gate pulse is applied at firing angle α. Current flows through R, L, and E from α to β. The back-EMF E opposes current flow, so there is a minimum firing angle αmin = sin⁻¹(E/Vm) below which the SCR cannot turn on.

The inductor causes current to lag, and extends conduction past the supply zero-crossing. The extinction angle β is found numerically by solving io(β) = 0. When SCR is off, it blocks current and the thyristor voltage equals vs − E.

🔌Circuit Components

AC Supply (Vs)

Single-phase sinusoidal source Vmsinωt. Range: 50–230 V. Pin 3 = positive terminal, Pin 4 = neutral/return.

SCR (Thyristor)

Main controlled switch. Pin 5 = Anode, Pin 6 = Cathode, Pin 7 = Gate. Fires at α (0°–150°), turns off at β when io = 0.

Gate Pulse Generator

Generates trigger pulse at firing angle α each cycle. Connected to SCR gate (pin 7) and VGP voltmeter.

Load — R, L, E

Series R-L-E load. R: 5–100 Ω, L: 10–200 mH, E: 0–0.8·Vm. L stores energy (extends β past π), E is back-EMF opposing current.

Ammeter (IL)

Measures load current io(t) in series. Pin 14 = +, Pin 15 = −. Connected between SCR cathode and load.

Voltmeters

VINP measures supply. VT measures voltage across SCR thyristor. VGP measures gate pulse. VLOAD measures load. VE measures back-EMF.

📈Output Waveforms

Input Voltage vin(t)
Full sinusoid Vmsinωt for 2 complete cycles. α and β markers shown each cycle.
Output Voltage vout(t)
Equals supply voltage Vmsinωt during conduction (α to β). Outside conduction, output equals E (back-EMF level). The yellow dashed line shows Vdc.
Thyristor Voltage vT(t)
During conduction: ≈0 V (small forward drop). Outside conduction: vT = vs − E = Vmsinωt − E. Positive when forward biased (before α), negative when reverse biased (after β).
Load Current io(t)
Rises from zero at α, peaks, decays to zero at β. Average Idc shown as dashed line.
Gate Pulse vg(t)
Narrow trigger pulses at α — one per cycle, two pulses across the 2-cycle view.

📊Simulation Output Parameters

Vdc
Average output voltage = (Vm/2π)(cosα − cosβ). Depends on both α and β.
Idc
Average load current = (Vdc − E) / R. Zero if Vdc ≤ E.
β (Extinction Angle)
Angle where current returns to zero. Found numerically. β > π when L is large.
Pdc and VE
Pdc = Idc × E = power delivered to back-EMF. VE reads E directly.
