Single Phase Half Wave Controlled Rectifier with R-L Load

Read the overview before starting — understand the experiment, components, waveforms and outputs.

Press Read Aloud to hear this section

🔬What is this experiment?

A Single-Phase Half Wave Controlled Rectifier with R-L Load converts single-phase AC into a controlled DC output using a single SCR (Silicon Controlled Rectifier). The load consists of a resistor R and inductor L in series.

Unlike a purely resistive load, the inductor stores energy and causes the SCR to keep conducting beyond the natural zero-crossing of the supply — up to the extinction angle β, where the instantaneous current finally returns to zero.

⚡How does it work?

The SCR fires when the supply voltage is positive and a gate pulse is applied at firing angle α. Current flows through R and L from α to β. The inductor opposes changes in current, causing it to lag and extend conduction past the supply zero-crossing (π). The extinction angle β is found numerically by solving io(β) = 0.

The conduction angle γ = β − α determines how long current flows each cycle. Larger L relative to R produces a wider conduction angle and smoother output current.

🎯Learning objectives

After completing this experiment you will understand: the working of a single-phase half-wave SCR rectifier with RL load, the effect of firing angle α on conduction and output characteristics, the role of inductance in extending conduction beyond π (delayed commutation), the concept of natural commutation in RL circuits, the shape of output voltage and load current waveforms, and how to calculate Vdc, Vrms, Idc, Irms, Form Factor, and Ripple Factor for an RL load circuit.

🔌Circuit Components

AC Supply (Vs)

Single-phase sinusoidal source Vmsinωt. Pin 3 = positive terminal, Pin 4 = neutral/return.

SCR (Thyristor)

Main controlled switch. Pin 5 = Anode, Pin 6 = Cathode, Pin 7 = Gate. Fires at α, turns off at β when io = 0.

Gate Pulse Generator

Generates trigger pulse at firing angle α each cycle. Connected to SCR gate (pin 7) and VGP voltmeter.

Load — R, L

Series R-L load. R dissipates power, L stores energy and extends conduction (β past π) due to inductive current freewheeling.

Ammeter (IL)

Measures load current io(t) in series. Pin 14 = +, Pin 15 = −. Connected between SCR cathode and load.

Voltmeters

VINP measures supply voltage. VT measures voltage across SCR. VGP measures gate pulse. VLOAD measures load voltage across R-L.

📈Output Waveforms

Input Voltage vin(t)
Full sinusoid Vmsinωt. Dashed lines mark α (purple) and β (blue) angles on the waveform.
Output Voltage vout(t)
Equals supply voltage during conduction (α to β). Zero otherwise. Yellow dashed line shows Vdc.
Thyristor Voltage vT(t)
Zero during conduction (α to β), equals supply voltage when SCR is off. Black waveform.
Load Current io(t)
Rises from zero at α, peaks near π, then decays to zero at β. Green dashed line shows Idc.
Gate Pulse vg(t)
Narrow trigger pulse applied at α each cycle. Fires the SCR to start conduction.

📊Simulation Output Parameters

Vdc
Average output voltage = (Vm/2π)(cosα − cosβ). Shown as yellow dashed line on output voltage graph.
Idc
Average load current = Vdc / R. Shown as green dashed line on current graph.
β (Extinction Angle)
Angle at which current returns to zero. Marked as blue dashed vertical line on all graphs.
γ (Conduction Angle)
γ = β − α. Represents the total duration of current flow per cycle.

Load Impedance (Z)
Z = √(R² + (ωL)²)
ω = 2π × 50 Hz = 314.16 rad/s
Impedance Angle (φ)
φ = tan⁻¹(ωL / R)
β > π when L > 0; β = π for pure R load
Instantaneous Current (RL)
io = (Vm/Z)[sin(ωt−φ) − sin(α−φ)·e−R(ωt−α)/(ωL)]
Valid for α ≤ ωt ≤ β; zero otherwise
Average Output Voltage (Vdc)
Vdc = (Vm/2π)(cos α − cos β)
β found numerically from io(β) = 0
Average Load Current (Idc)
Idc = Vdc / R
No back-EMF; all voltage drops across R
Output Power (Pout)
Pout = Irms² × R
Power dissipated entirely in the resistance R
